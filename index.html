Colorado earned time & parole eligibility

Earned time

Earned time reduces a sentence at a fixed monthly rate and may never reduce a sentence by more than 30% total (C.R.S. 17-22.5-405(4)(a)). The monthly rate depends on the offense:

  • 10 days/month — class 1–3 felonies; level 1–2 drug felonies.
  • 14 days/month — class 4–6 felonies; level 3–4 drug felonies.
  • 12 days/month — offenders otherwise in the 14-day tier, if they have a current or prior conviction for felony motor vehicle theft; a victim-rights felony under C.R.S. 24-4.1-302(1); or an offense under C.R.S. 18-3-303, 18-3-305, 18-3-306, 18-6-701, 18-7-402 through 18-7-407, 18-12-102, or 18-12-109.

Two additional, uncapped categories sit outside the 30% ceiling: achievement earned time (C.R.S. 17-22.5-405(9)) for program milestones and exceptional conduct, and credential/education earned time (C.R.S. 17-22.5-405(3.7)) for degrees earned by nonviolent offenders.

Parole eligibility

Eligibility is a percentage of the imposed sentence. Whether earned time advances that date depends on the tier.

  • 50%, less earned time — class 2–6 felonies, level 1–4 drug felonies, and unclassified felonies (C.R.S. 17-22.5-403(1)). Class 1 felonies are not parole-eligible.
  • 75%, less earned time — second degree murder, first degree assault, first degree kidnapping (unless class 1), first degree arson, first degree burglary, or aggravated robbery, committed 7/1/2004–12/31/2024 (C.R.S. 17-22.5-403(2.5)). Applies only to the class 2 or 3 versions of these crimes, or the class 4 or 5 versions where the person has a prior crime of violence.
  • 85%, no earned-time reduction — second degree murder, first degree assault, class 2 felony kidnapping, sexual assault under part 4 of article 3 of title 18, first degree arson, first degree burglary, or aggravated robbery, committed on/after 1/1/2025 (C.R.S. 17-22.5-303.3(1.5)). With two prior crime-of-violence convictions, the person serves the full sentence before parole (C.R.S. 17-22.5-303.3(2.5)).

How credits apply

  • Regular earned time (405(1)/(1.5)) advances both the parole eligibility date and the mandatory release date. It is capped at 30% and reduces the eligibility date only in the 50% and 75% tiers — not the 85%/100% tier.
  • Achievement and credential earned time (405(9), (3.7)) are additional credits exempt from the 30% cap. They do not apply to offenders in a tier that allows no earned-time reduction.
  • Good time does not advance the parole eligibility date for felonies committed on/after 7/1/1985; the 50% eligibility baseline replaced it. It factors into the mandatory release date, not the eligibility date.
